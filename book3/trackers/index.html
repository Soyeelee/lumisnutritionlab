The rules

Page 68 of the book, word for word. They’re printed on your tracker page too.

WHAT TO WATCH FOR
Salty snacks — chips, pretzels, popcorn, ramen, pizza. Anything that makes your tongue go whoa.
HOW TO OBSERVE
This one is about your body’s bell. Every time you eat something salty, count how many times you actually drink water. Notice your mouth and your head.
HOW TO RECORD
Write what you ate, how many times you drank water, and what you noticed. Not sure it was thirst? A question mark is a real answer.

A blank day is not a failed day — you don’t need something to write every day. — L.

Testing one thing?

Lumi’s rule on page 47 is “Change one thing. Only one.” Choose a change that only makes a day gentler — water with the salty snack, a smaller bowl, a check-in halfway (Maya’s mom suggests the first two on page 2) — never “more” salt to see what happens, and decide it together.

Run it together

From For the Grown-Up Lab Partner, pages 72–74.

1 · Ask. Don’t check. The record belongs to your child. An inspected notebook stops being honest by about day three.

2 · Praise the blank day. A child who writes nothing has produced an honest result — harder and rarer than a full row. Answer it with: “Good — you left it empty because it was empty. That’s the whole job.”

3 · Let a wrong reason stand for a minute. Most children will name a cause — usually the loudest one. Correct it and you hand over an answer, not a method.

Know when to stop. One week, salty things only. If it becomes a log of everything eaten, or your child asks if a number is too high, close it and come back in a year or two. Curiosity doesn’t need a streak.

When to see a doctor instead

This book is about ordinary, passing sensations. Call your pediatrician if headaches come daily, wake your child from sleep, or keep getting worse; if thirst and bathroom trips rise sharply alongside unusual tiredness or weight loss; or if your child already lives with a diagnosed condition. Go now for a sudden severe headache with confusion, vomiting or a stiff neck. Nothing here is medical advice.
