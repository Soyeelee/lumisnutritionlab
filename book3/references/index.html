What the evidence does — and doesn’t — show

  • Salt and thirst: A big salty snack makes blood a little saltier. Special cells in the brain notice, switch on thirst and tell the kidneys to save water. That’s textbook body science; the measurements come from adults.
  • Thirst comes early: For healthy people on an ordinary day, thirst usually shows up before the body is short of water, so “by the time you’re thirsty, it’s too late” isn’t right. That conclusion comes from a review about adults, and hard exercise in hot weather is a different situation.
  • Headaches: Some people get a headache when they go a long time without water, but many don’t, and scientists don’t yet know exactly why it happens. Headaches have lots of causes, so a headache doesn’t always mean “drink.”
  • Noticing: When attention is busy elsewhere, body feelings like tiredness or pain feel weaker, but they don’t switch off. People also differ in how quickly they feel thirsty. The studies we found on practising noticing were all in adults, and the gains were small and slow.
  • One afternoon, not a diet: This book is about one afternoon. Eating a lot of salt day after day is linked with higher blood pressure, in children as well as adults. That is a separate question the book deliberately leaves alone.

Corrections to the Lab Notes and Word Lab

Ten passages in Lumi’s Lab Notes and Word Lab simplify too far, or say something the evidence doesn’t support. Here is what each one says in the book, and what it should say. The rest stands.

SYMPTOM page 62

In the book: A clue your body shows on the outside about something happening on the inside.

It should say: A clue you feel on the inside that something is going on in your body. Only you can feel it.

That’s how doctors use the word; a clue other people can see, like a rash or a fever, is called a sign. Sources 28, 29 below.

ATTENTION page 63

In the book: …you have zero left for noticing what’s happening in your own body.

It should say: …you have less left for noticing what’s happening in your own body.

Busy attention turns body feelings down, not off, in studies of adults and of children. Sources 15, 16, 17 below.

DEHYDRATION (Lumi’s note) page 61

In the book: Jiwoo didn’t notice until too late.

It should say: Jiwoo didn’t notice for a long time.

Thirst usually comes before the body is short of water, as page 74 says. Source 8 below.

EXPERIMENT (Lumi’s note) page 56

In the book: The movie was the experiment I didn’t design but definitely observed.

It should say: The movie wasn’t an experiment — nobody changed one thing on purpose. I just observed.

An experiment changes one thing on purpose; watching is observing. Sources 30, 15 below.

Lab Notes 1 (graph) page 41

In the book: SALT IN MY BLOOD · “This is the headache, right on time.”

It should say: SALT I ATE · “This is where my head started to hurt.”

Blood always has salt, and the body keeps it nearly steady; headaches don’t come on a schedule. Sources 1, 2, 11 below.

Lab Notes 2 page 42

In the book: A tight, thirsty cell is what a tight-hat head is made of.

It should say: Special cells in your brain notice the change and ring the thirst bell. Why do some heads ache when they need water? Scientists are still finding out.

How going without water causes headaches is not yet understood. Sources 1, 11 below.

Lab Notes 3 page 43

In the book: It rings as a dry mouth. A tight hat. An ache behind your eyes.

It should say: You feel it as wanting a drink, often with a dry mouth. Jiwoo’s head ached too, and a headache can have lots of causes.

Thirst is the urge to drink; a headache is a separate clue that only some people get. Sources 1, 11, 12, 14 below.

Lab Notes 4 page 44

In the book: Either way, that leaves exactly zero for your own dry mouth.

It should say: Either way, there’s less left over for your own dry mouth.

Same as ATTENTION. Sources 15, 16, 17 below.

Lab Notes 6 page 47

In the book: Four were true. Only one was the reason.

It should say: Four were true. They didn’t all matter the same.

Everything you drink since your last bathroom trip, and how long you wait, adds up. Sources 1, 22 below.

Lab Notes 7 page 48

In the book: A quiet bell can be practiced.

It should say: Listening for a quiet bell can be practiced.

Practice may help you notice; nothing shows it changes the signal itself. Sources 20, 21 below.
